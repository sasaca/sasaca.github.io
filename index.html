No. 01Enterprise master data

iDOT PIM

An AI-assisted master data platform with enterprise-grade approval workflows, compliance routing, and segregation of duties.

iDOT manages the three core enterprise master-data domains — vendor, customer, and product — from first request to approved record. Every change runs through a configurable approval workflow with SLA tracking, audit timelines, and compliance branches for LATAM, EU, SOX, and GxP rules. It is the exact discipline I design for Fortune 500 clients, implemented as working software.

  • Context-aware workflow engine inserts approval steps at runtime: corporate security review for LATAM vendors, finance sign-off for the EU, supervisor review whenever SOX-sensitive fields like bank accounts are touched.
  • Segregation of duties enforced in code — no one can approve their own request, including the master-data team itself.
  • Claude-powered document AI: upload a W-9 or VAT certificate and the onboarding form fills itself, with an "AI-populated" badge on every field for the audit trail.
  • Weighted fuzzy matching across name, tax ID, DUNS, and address stops duplicate vendors before they are ever created.
  • Roughly 21,700 lines of code — 109 routes, 27 database tables, 13 demo roles — built in ten days.

No. 02Data cleansing for ERP

Lustro

AI-powered master-data cleansing that turns dirty vendor lists into SAP-loadable files — with a human in the loop only when confidence is low.

ERP migrations still run on consultants hand-cleansing spreadsheets for weeks. Lustro takes messy vendor, customer, product, or finance master data, cleanses and standardizes it, and outputs the exact load format the target ERP requires — SAP S/4HANA first, with Oracle and Dynamics 365 profiles. A deterministic rules engine handles the predictable majority; AI touches only the ambiguous residue under a hard $2-per-job budget cap.

  • Generates the real SAP Migration Cockpit package — the per-structure CSV-ZIP format most teams assume is a simple Excel file. It isn't, and Lustro builds it correctly.
  • Sanctions screening built in: vendors and customers checked against 71,095 bundled OFAC and EU entries with two-tier exact and fuzzy matching.
  • Tamper-evident by design: an append-only, hash-chained audit log enforced by a database trigger, with strict per-customer data isolation.
  • A hard safety guardrail: fuzzy matches never auto-merge, so a confidently wrong merge of two legal entities can never reach the ERP.
  • Live with self-serve signup and a free tier — with Stripe billing and two-factor login built in, about 15,500 lines across a rules engine, API, batch worker, and React app.

The Veil Strand

A solo-built fishing life-sim where every story beat is something you catch — run with studio-grade data discipline.

My dream game, in active development for Steam. You inherit a tackle shop in Carillon, a harbor town that gives its unbearable memories to the sea; on rare "Silvered tides" the water turns mirror-still and you can fish the past back, one fragment at a time. Method

Rules I build by.

Five independent systems kept teaching the same lessons. At some point they stopped being habits and became laws — each one enforced by a named mechanism, not a slogan.

  • A false merge is worse than a missed duplicate. Lustro and ViveGuate only auto-merge records on exact identifiers. Every fuzzy match — however confident — routes to a human, so a wrong merge of two legal entities can never reach the ERP or the map.
  • No one approves their own request — including us. iDOT's segregation-of-duties engine blocks self-approval in code, even for the master-data team that operates the platform.
  • AI fails open, never silently wrong. Every AI feature in ViveGuate is forced through schema-validated tool use, checked against the app's own taxonomy, and degrades to non-AI behavior on any error or missing key.
  • No balance number lives in code, ever. The Veil Strand keeps every gameplay number in versioned data files behind hard-fail schema validation that names the offending row. Tuning the game never means touching the engine.
  • The audit log must be unable to lie. Lustro's append-only, hash-chained audit trail is enforced by a database trigger — application code can't soften it, and tampering breaks the chain visibly.
  • An AI budget is a design constraint, not a hope. Lustro hard-caps AI spend per job; when the meter runs out it degrades to flagging instead of fixing. Costs are engineered, not discovered on the invoice.
